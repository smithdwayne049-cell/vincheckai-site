Clutch replacement cost

Clutch replacement in the United States usually costs about $750 to $2,500 for parts and labor, and many everyday cars land between $1,200 and $1,800. All-wheel-drive, luxury and performance cars can cost $1,500 to $3,000. Labor is often half or more of the bill because the transmission must come out.

Estimates for United States, November 2026. Real prices depend on the car, the region and the shop.

What changes the price

  • Car type: AWD, luxury and performance cars cost more.
  • Dual-mass flywheels are costly and often cannot be resurfaced.
  • Labor: the transmission has to be removed, which takes many hours.
  • Whether the flywheel and release bearing are replaced at the same time.
  • Shop type and local labor rates, often $75 to $125 per hour or more.
  • Parts brand: OEM kits cost more than aftermarket kits.

How not to overpay

  • Ask the shop to confirm the clutch is really worn, not a hydraulic or cable fault that costs far less.
  • Ask to replace the flywheel work together with the clutch, which avoids paying labor twice.
  • Ask for an itemized quote that lists parts and labor separately.
  • Get a second quote from another shop before you agree.
  • Ask for the exact kit brand and whether it is OEM.

FAQ

How much does a clutch replacement cost?

In the US most clutch replacements cost about $750 to $2,500, parts and labor included. Many mainstream cars are between $1,200 and $1,800.

Why is clutch replacement so expensive?

The mechanic has to remove the transmission to reach the clutch, so labor is half or more of the total. The clutch kit itself is a smaller part of the bill.

How long does a clutch last?

A clutch often lasts many years, but driving style and traffic make a big difference. Slipping, a high pedal bite or a burning smell are common signs it is wearing out.

Can I replace only the clutch disc?

Shops usually recommend a full kit, with pressure plate and release bearing, because the labor is the same and it avoids a repeat job.
